CHOREO converts heterogeneous skill sources—including motion datasets, reinforcement learning policies, and generative models—into unified SkillMotion assets. Given a user instruction, an LLM planner selects and sequences these assets, while the CHOREO runtime executes them as long-horizon skill compositions on the Unitree G1.

Abstract

Recent advances in humanoid robotics have produced diverse skills through reinforcement learning, motion imitation, and generative modeling. Yet these capabilities remain siloed because they are built around incompatible representations, interfaces, and controllers. We present CHOREO, a framework for training-free composition of heterogeneous humanoid skills. Our key observation is that, regardless of how a skill is learned, it can ultimately be expressed as an executable motion trajectory. CHOREO converts each capability into SkillMotion, a unified representation that combines motion states, contacts, semantics, and boundary conditions. Skills are composed through direct continuation, cubic Hermite blending, or validated bridge motions, without retraining source models or updating models at test time. On Unitree G1 in MuJoCo, CHOREO organizes 2,950 admitted SkillMotion assets derived from heterogeneous sources and achieves 95.4% sequence success across 130 multi-action tasks, including 93.8% success on eight-action sequences.

Long-Horizon Skill Composition

CHOREO composes skills from heterogeneous sources into continuous, long-horizon behaviors without retraining the original models or low-level policies.

CHOREO Framework

CHOREO separates offline registration from online composition. Source-specific motion outputs are converted into canonical trajectories, annotated, and validated before entering the SkillMotion library. At runtime, an LLM planner produces an ordered skill sequence; CHOREO resolves the assets, selects compatible trackers, constructs boundary transitions, and executes the composed reference through a shared interface.

SkillMotion and Adaptive Transitions

SkillMotion augments a canonical trajectory with semantic labels, contact states, execution constraints, boundary descriptors, embodiment metadata, source provenance, and validation evidence. This common representation decouples skill generation from execution and lets independently developed capabilities share the same runtime.

For consecutive skills, CHOREO first evaluates their aligned boundary states. Compatible motions continue directly; nearby boundaries are connected with cubic Hermite interpolation; larger gaps retrieve a bridge motion whose entry and exit are validated before execution.

Results

CHOREO completes 124 of 130 fixed multi-action sequences. Its advantage grows with sequence length: it achieves 93.8% success on eight-action tasks, compared with 41.7% for the strongest baseline. Across 552 planned skill boundaries, CHOREO reaches 96.7% switch success while reducing the fall rate to 4.6%.
